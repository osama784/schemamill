# Transaction groups — partition, render, verify

**Status:** built · **Last updated:** 2026-10-03

A migration plan now says what applies as one unit. `Plan` carries a deterministic partition of its steps into transaction groups, `renderSql` wraps each transactional group in `BEGIN;`/`COMMIT;` and stands a non-transactional step alone, `plan` labels the groups, and the gated live-PostgreSQL suite proves atomic rollback and its falsification. The settled decisions are on [#30](https://github.com/osama784/schemamill/issues/30) (design review rounds 1–2 and the approved brief), the slice's ledger; the first real non-transactional step kinds and the multi-group display wording, named as out of scope here, have since shipped in the [constraints and indexes slice](./constraints-indexes.md) ([#33](https://github.com/osama784/schemamill/issues/33)). The durable decision — boundaries follow step semantics, never user configuration — is recorded in [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-transaction-boundaries-follow-step-semantics.md).

## Scope

**In:** `Plan.groups: readonly TransactionGroup[]`; `TransactionGroup = { start, end, transactional }` — half-open indices into `steps`, with non-empty groups tiling `0..steps.length` in order and `transactional: false` marking a standalone group that applies outside a transaction; the compile-time exhaustive `TRANSACTIONAL: Record<Step['kind'], boolean>` classification beside the step union, now covering all twenty-three kinds; the coalescing function `groupSteps` and its predicate parameter as the test seam; `plan()` computing the groups once; `renderSql` walking the groups and wrapping each transactional one; the `plan` command's transaction-labeled headers, single-group and multi-group; and the always-on live rollback proof and its falsification.

**Out, named — never silent:** group separators beyond the one blank line between groups, SQL comments, and any label richer than the header and section lines. The multi-group display wording and the first real non-transactional step kinds, named here when this slice landed, have since shipped in the [constraints and indexes slice](./constraints-indexes.md) ([#33](https://github.com/osama784/schemamill/issues/33)).

**Deliberately shallow for now:** classification is one fixed boolean per step kind, not derived from the statement text and not PostgreSQL-version-aware; hazards, step order, and step indices are untouched, so the partition is metadata beside the steps, never an ordering input. A plan is multi-group only when a concurrent index kind stands in it; every other kind stays transactional, and a plan without one remains exactly one covering group.

## The rule

Transaction safety is a property of a step's kind, decided beside the step union. `TRANSACTIONAL` is a `Record<Step['kind'], boolean>` — compile-time exhaustive, so adding a kind to the union without classifying it fails the build — and all twenty-three current kinds are covered: the two concurrent index kinds, `create-index-concurrently` and `drop-index-concurrently`, are `false`, and the other twenty-one are transactional — `create-table`, `drop-table`, `add-column`, `drop-column`, `alter-column`, `add-identity`, `drop-identity`, `alter-identity`, `add-primary-key`, `drop-primary-key`, `add-foreign-key`, `drop-foreign-key`, `add-unique-constraint`, `drop-unique-constraint`, `add-check-constraint`, `drop-check-constraint`, `create-index`, `drop-index`, `create-sequence`, `drop-sequence`, and `alter-sequence`.

`groupSteps` walks the steps in order and partitions them: consecutive transaction-safe steps coalesce into one group, while a non-transactional step becomes a group of its own, even beside another non-transactional step. Every group is non-empty, the groups tile `0..steps.length` in order, an empty step list yields no groups, and the result depends only on the step order and the classification. `plan()` computes the partition once, from the fixed classification, and returns it beside the steps. A plan without a concurrent index kind is exactly one transactional group covering every step; one concurrent build splits the plan into wrapped groups around a bare standalone group, and two adjacent builds stand alone separately. The predicate parameter on `groupSteps` remains the seam that lets tests exercise synthetic classifications.

## SQL format

`renderSql` walks `plan.groups` in order rather than `plan.steps`: a transactional group emits `BEGIN;` before and `COMMIT;` after its statements, and a standalone group renders its statements bare. One statement per line, one trailing newline — a multi-group plan separates its groups with exactly one blank line, and a single-group plan is byte-identical to before. An empty plan still renders `''`, the `renderSql(plan)` signature is unchanged, and the plan is still the only input: the renderer invents no boundaries and reads no environment.

## CLI output

`formatPlan` labels the step list `N steps in one transaction:` when the plan's groups are exactly one transactional group covering every step — singular `1 step in one transaction:` — and `N steps outside a transaction:` when its only group is non-transactional. A multi-group plan reads `N steps in M groups:` with one section line per group — `  group k of M:`, or `  group k of M (standalone):` when the group is non-transactional — each group's steps indented four spaces and numbered continuously across the plan. The plain `N steps:` header remains the defensive fallback for a step list with no groups. An empty plan stays `No changes.`. Step numbering, kind padding, and every step line otherwise are unchanged: only the header and section lines gained the transaction structure.

## Verification

Four levels pin the slice:

- **Unit tests** — `packages/core/src/plan.test.ts` pins the classification (all twenty-three kinds listed, the twenty-one transactional asserted `true` and the two concurrent kinds `false`, their keys exactly `TRANSACTIONAL`'s), coalescing with a synthetic classification (`[T, T, F, T]` → `[{ 0, 2, true }, { 2, 3, false }, { 3, 4, true }]`), tiling and non-emptiness, an all-transactional step list as one group, an all-standalone classification as one group per step, an empty step list as no groups, and the plan-level partition; `assertPlan` computes the expected groups for every existing plan case.
- **Render tests and goldens** — `packages/postgres/src/render.test.ts` pins the empty plan as `''`, a non-empty plan as exactly one `BEGIN;`/`COMMIT;` pair, and a three-group plan whose standalone group renders bare between two wrapped groups with one blank line between groups; the six single-group goldens in `packages/postgres/test/goldens/*.sql` gained their wrapper lines, and `multi-group.sql` and `index-concurrent.sql` pin the blank-line-separated multi-group form.
- **CLI tests and goldens** — `packages/cli/src/format.test.ts` pins the singular, plural, single-standalone, multi-group, defensive plain-header, and empty cases; the existing five `packages/cli/test/goldens/*.plan.txt` were re-pinned to `N steps in one transaction:` when the slice landed, and the constraints-and-indexes scene's golden pins `13 steps in 4 groups:` with two standalone sections.
- **Live harness** — the scene corpus stays green under wrapped SQL, and three always-on tests in the gated suite (`SCHEMAMILL_TEST_PG_URL`) carry the proofs:
  - **Rollback proof** — `primary-key-add`, the corpus's smallest corruptible shape at two steps (`alter-column` then `add-primary-key`), is rendered, its last statement retargeted at `public.t_missing` with exactly one rendered line changed, and applied with `psql` `ON_ERROR_STOP=1`; the failure must name the missing relation, and the re-dump after it must import to an exactly empty diff against the baseline — the statement that succeeded before the failure left no partial effect.
  - **Falsification** — the same corrupted SQL with every `BEGIN;`/`COMMIT;` line stripped (exactly one pair pinned) is applied to a freshly rebuilt baseline: the same relation must fail, and the earlier statement's effect must still be readable — `public.t.id` stays `NOT NULL`. The two runs differ only in the wrapper lines, so the rollback proof's empty diff is attributable to them and the wrappers stay load-bearing.
  - **Concurrent falsification** — the `index-concurrently` scene's rendered `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` statement, pinned as isolated by blank lines and exactly one in the plan, is wrapped in `BEGIN;`/`COMMIT;` and applied to a freshly built baseline: PostgreSQL refuses it with `cannot run inside a transaction block` and leaves no index behind, while the same statement bare builds a valid index. The two runs differ only in the wrapper lines, so the standalone group is load-bearing.
  - Both falsifications pin their statement and group shape first, so a planner or renderer change cannot quietly turn the proof into a no-op.

## Done means

- The classification test names all twenty-three kinds, and every unit test above passes.
- Every rendered golden's wrappers match its groups — single-group goldens exactly one pair, and `multi-group.sql` and `index-concurrent.sql` bare standalone statements between blank-line-separated wrapped groups — and every CLI plan golden carries a transaction header.
- The live rollback proof, its falsification, and the concurrent falsification pass against live PostgreSQL on the gated suite, and every scene still round-trips with an exactly empty diff.
- `pnpm build`, `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm test`, and `pnpm format:check` pass from the repo root.

## Non-goals

The `--sql` flag; user-controllable commit points or transaction flags; PostgreSQL-version detection or conditional wrapping; DML; expand/contract orchestration; per-phase or per-step commit boundaries; `\set ON_ERROR_STOP`; and apply tooling — [ADR 0001](../adr/0001-generate-never-apply.md) stands, so the generated SQL keeps its wrappers, and whether and how to set `ON_ERROR_STOP` stays the user's call. Multi-group display wording, group separators, and the first real non-transactional step kind, deferred when this slice landed, have since shipped in the [constraints and indexes slice](./constraints-indexes.md): separators are exactly one blank line between groups, and no further decoration is planned. `compare` and `plan --verbose` are unchanged.
