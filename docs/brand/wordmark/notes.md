# Kit — wordmark: the custom-details pass

The wordmark's planned custom details (BRIEF §4/§10, v3 notes' letterform round) are developed
here as **one stock control + two treatment candidates**, each shipped as outlines-only SVG in
both colorways, all built by `_tooling/` from the OFL-licensed IBM Plex Sans Medium skeleton.

Everything in this folder is deterministic: `node build.mjs --wordmark <id>` rebuilds it; the
lockups in `../masters/` follow the same flag. Rebuilds are byte-identical (verified by hashing
two consecutive builds; a flag flip changes only the wordmark-dependent files).

## The control

`wordmark-stock-light/dark.svg` is the exact v3 setting: outlined Plex Medium (500), glyph by
glyph, tracking +0.005 em, **glyph data byte-identical to the shipped `03-the-chute` lockups**
(checked in `_tooling/check.mjs` against `lockup-horizontal-light.svg`). It exists so the
treatments can be judged against the baseline — it is not a candidate.

## What the measurements found (before designing anything)

Rendered and measured from the outlined paths (`_tooling/explore.mjs`; numbers at fs=100).
The rejected-option renders are regenerated by `_tooling/details-lab.mjs` (the v3 `hatch-test.mjs`
pattern: the evidence for a rejection stays reproducible).

| measurement | value |
| --- | --- |
| glyphs | s c h e m a m i l l |
| ink gaps between letters | s→c 9.3 · c→h 10.9 · h→e 12.1 · e→m 12.6 · m→a 11.8 · a→m 11.2 · **m→i 14.6 · i→l 15.1** · l→l 10.3 |
| stem width | 10.9 (uniform across stems) |
| `m` internals | 3 stems of 10.9, counters 20.2 wide, uniform internal pitch 31.1 |
| x-height / ascender ink | 53.2 / 74.0 |
| `i` dot | 12.9 × 13.1 rounded square (r ≈ 3.4) |
| `l` foot | 7.2 wide × 8.9 tall shelf behind a compound elbow of 10.8 drop |
| `a` foot | 6.1 wide × 8.7 tall, same family |

Two facts drove the design:

1. **There is no adjacent `mm` pair in `schemamill`.** The two m's are separated by `a` (`m·a·m`),
   so v3's suggested "`mm` pair join" cannot be a ligature; the word's one true adjacent
   identical pair is the final **`ll`** — the terminator. The planned detail moves there by
   evidence, and this is the correction recorded for the letterform round.
2. **The word's loosest intervals are the two around the `i`** (14.6 / 15.1 against a median of
   11.8 over the nine stock ink gaps): the `mill` ending drifts and the `i` floats. Both
   treatments therefore carry a shared *registration* (i −3.0, ll −3.5).

What the registration actually measures **on the shipped outlines** (ink-box gaps, identical in
both tones; re-derivable with `node explore.mjs gaps`):

| pair | stock | finish | squarecut | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `m→i` | 14.60 | **11.60** | **11.60** | lands on the word's rhythm (median 11.80) |
| `i→l` | 15.10 | 14.60 | 14.60 | still the word's loosest gap |
| `l→l` | 10.30 | 13.30 | 12.30 | the trimmed feet open the pair |
| nine-gap median | 11.80 | 11.80 | 11.80 | |

The registration was intended to pull the `mill` ending back to the word's texture; what it
measures is narrower than that: **`m→i` lands at 11.60, but `i→l` only moves 15.10 → 14.60**
(the `i`'s −3.0 also widens `i→l` by 3.0, so the `ll`'s −3.5 nets only −0.5), and **the
terminator's trimmed feet open `l→l` from 10.30 to 12.30 (squarecut) / 13.30 (finish)**. The
ending is no longer the tightest pair anywhere — that is the `s→c` start at 9.30.

***Open question (recorded, not papered over; geometry unchanged):*** `i→l` remains at 14.60 —
looser than the nine-gap median (11.80) and the word's loosest interval — so at large sizes the
`mill` ending can still read a touch open between the `i` and the `l`. A future pass could pull
the `l` glyphs (or use a per-pair registration) to close it; that changes the ending's texture
beyond the treatment as chosen, so it is left open rather than silently tuned.

## The two treatments

Both are pure path surgery on the same outlines (`_tooling/treatments.mjs`); the builders
reproduce the stock glyphs byte-for-byte at their defaults (asserted by `check.mjs`).

### finish — the restrained pass

* terminator feet trimmed **7.2 → 4.2** (trim 3.0 @fs100); Plex's elbow stays exactly as drawn;
* registration: i −3.0, ll −3.5.

The wordmark's ending stops stepping off to the right; nothing else about Plex's voice changes.
In the ×5 magnified strips at 224 px wide the trim is visible on inspection; at 120 px all three
settings converge (the foot's mass is ~2 px either way) and finish is indistinguishable from
stock. Zero risk: no new geometry, only a shorter existing feature.

### squarecut — the decisive pass (recommended)

* **every foot in the word is stepped**: the `l` feet and the `a` foot get a 2.5 fillet and the
  edge runs **straight to the baseline** — the terminal becomes a machined step instead of the
  soft compound elbow; the terminator's feet are additionally trimmed 2.0 (`l`) / 1.5 (`a`);
* registration: same as `finish` (i −3.0, ll −3.5).

Why this is the one to ship: the mark's own device is *square out* — the raw rods enter with
round mill-finish ends and the emitted rows leave as plain rectangles. The wordmark is the
refined output, and its three feet are the only place the system can be carried into the
letterforms without touching the skeleton's rhythm. The step reads as a deliberate hand-cut
("a handmade touch", not a redraw): at 1200 px the `l` feet are visibly machined; at 120 px the
shape simply renders as a solid foot — **no mush, no thinning, no loss at any size** (a squared
corner cannot anti-alias away the way a thin feature can). Fallback risk is bounded: the letter
is still unmistakably Plex's `l` (same stem, same shelf, same x-height); only the corner curve
is gone.

**Recommendation: `squarecut`.** It gives the kit one recognizable crafted gesture that belongs
to the mark's system; `finish` is the more conservative pull and stays one flag away. I would
switch to `finish` if you want the wordmark to be as invisible as possible next to the mark, or
if a type-fluent reviewer objects to the altered `l` terminal on principle.

## Tested and rejected (evidence kept)

| option | what it is | verdict / why not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| plinth | `l` shelf extended under the pair so both l's sit on one base | reads as a **"U"** at any size — it changes the letters' identity (`_proof/cand-ll.png`, row 7) |
| shelf-less elbow | remove the shelf, keep the elbow; the terminal ends in the curve | the ending goes **soft** — the opposite of the mark's "square out" (row 5) |
| plain stem | `l` as a bare rectangle, foot and elbow both removed | renders as two austere rectangles — it *substitutes* the letterform (a Helvetica-style `l`) rather than finishing Plex's own; cold next to the word's curves (row 6) |
| trim 4.8 | finish taken to the nib | the foot becomes a weak nib; the letter reads unstable at large sizes (row 4) |
| trim 2.0 / 3.6 | lighter trims | both fine; 3.0 chosen as the largest trim that still leaves a *foot* rather than a nib |
| dot: square / stem-wide / round | redraw the `i` dot | invisible at real sizes (the dot is 13 units at fs 100) and it makes the wordmark read as *a different font's* dot; rejected (`_proof/dot-A/B.png`) |

## Small-size and large-size behaviour (rendered evidence)

All proof renders are resvg with `loadSystemFonts: false` — **no fonts exist in the renderer**,
so the files' font-independence is proven by every image. Nearest-neighbour magnified strips from
true-size renders (PIL, `_tooling/strip.py`):

| what | evidence |
| --- | --- |
| treatments at reading scale (224 px wide, ×5) | `_proof/truth-wordmark-comp-light/dark.png` — rows: stock, finish, squarecut |
| default treatment, true pixels 480 → 64 px (×3) | `_proof/truth-wordmark-light/dark.png` |
| full-size renders (1200 px) | `_proof/wordmark-<treatment>-1200-<tone>.png` |

Honest size behaviour: the details are a **large-size tell** — readable in the ×5 strips at
224 px wide, gone at 120 px, where all three settings are effectively identical (like the mark's
rod caps, which are a ≥64 px tell). Nothing about the treatments costs legibility at any size,
and none of them thins at small sizes.

## Risks (honest)

1. **`squarecut` edits the letterform of an OFL skeleton** — permitted (outlines are derivative
   work; the OFL's reserved-name condition governs redistribution of modified *font software*,
   and no font software is embedded or redistributed here), but the wordmark must never be
   described as "IBM Plex unmodified" once this ships. `finish`, by contrast, is a pure
   reduction of an existing feature.
2. A type-fluent viewer can spot the squared `l` terminal; that is the intent (a hand-cut), but
   it is a taste call, and the stock control ships alongside for exactly that reason.
3. The registration (i −3.0 / ll −3.5) closes `m→i` to 11.60 — the word's rhythm (median 11.80) —
   but leaves `i→l` at 14.60, the word's loosest gap, and the trimmed feet widen `l→l`
   (10.30 → 12.30 / 13.30). At very large sizes a viewer who knows Plex's default metrics may
   read the ending as "moved" in more than one direction at once. That is what the shipped
   outlines measure; the open question is recorded above, not hidden.
4. If the wordmark ever changes family, the treatments must be re-derived — they are drawn from
   Plex's measured geometry (stems 10.9, foot 7.2 × 8.9), not from a portable spec.

## Rebuilding

```bash
cd _tooling
npm i                          # opentype.js + @resvg/resvg-js (node_modules is not shipped)
node build.mjs                 # default wordmark (squarecut) → ../wordmark + ../masters + _proof
node build.mjs --wordmark finish   # flag flip; regenerates the lockups too (byte-identical if repeated)
node check.mjs                 # structure + provenance checks (429 checks)
node verify-ondisk.mjs         # render the shipped files and compare with the proofs
node details-lab.mjs           # regenerate every comparison render quoted above
python3 strip.py wordmark && python3 strip.py comp   # re-make the magnified truth strips
```
